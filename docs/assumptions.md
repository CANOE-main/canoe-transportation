# Assumptions

!!! note "How to read this reference"
    - This reference records source limitations, data gaps, and the assumptions used to address them. Tables are organized by technology scope. The tags `#edge-case`, `#legacy-change`, and `#multi-parameter` identify exceptions, changes from the legacy backend, and assumptions shared across parameters. Review markers flag content for review; they do not imply that it is incorrect or accepted. 
    - Source identities and citations are in the [source registry](../config/sources.yaml). The [parameter rules index](../config/parameters/rules.yaml) points to extraction and harmonization rules, while reviewed manual tables supply additional inputs. The [default scenario](../config/scenarios/default.yaml) records the default run selections.

LDVs comprise cars and passenger/freight light trucks. Medium/heavy trucks exclude buses unless named; the source-family term MHDV can include buses. Road vehicles include buses and motorcycles. Non-road modes are air, rail and marine; off-road equipment is a separate modeled category. BEVs are battery EVs; PHEVs are plug-in hybrids; PEVs include both. BCT combines British Columbia and the territories.

CS/CD mean charge-sustaining/charge-depleting operation; UF is the distance utility factor, HHV higher heating value, C2A capacity-to-activity, and M&R maintenance and repair. GVWR is a vehicle's rated fully loaded weight. [*CER CEF*](https://www.cer-rec.gc.ca/en/data-analysis/canada-energy-future/) denotes Canada's Energy Future. [*Ontario MTO*](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/vehicle-population-data) denotes Ministry of Transportation Ontario. Source years, model vintages and monetary dollar years are distinct.

| Technology class | Source / challenge | Assumption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cars and passenger light trucks | GDP indexing does not represent their service divergence. | `future_car_demand: extrapolated` uses the same observed CAGR estimator (excluding COVID years) and YAML controls as [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) `base_year` extensions, then anchors at the actual selected-edition car-activity observation. | Non-road modes | `#multi-parameter` [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) has provincial energy but national service intensity. | Estimate activity using the same-year national intensity. Air demand includes aviation gasoline, although incumbent [existing capacity](#existing_capacity) excludes it as it is near-negligible. |
| Transport services | [*CER CEF*](https://www.cer-rec.gc.ca/en/data-analysis/canada-energy-future/) provides Canada-wide real GDP. | Anchor each province's demand at the actual selected-edition [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) observation, then multiply by `real GDP(target year) / real GDP(observation year)`, including the model `base_year` and the period-start `legacy` or period-end `prospective` evaluation year. [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) constant/extrapolated endpoint policies do not change demand's anchor. Provincial GDP differences are unrepresented. |

!!! tip "GDP-indexed projections ignore vehicle class demand switching"
    With `future_car_demand: GDP-indexed`, car and passenger-light-truck demand grow at the same rate, so their shares of combined passenger-LDV demand remain constant within each region. The `extrapolated` mode projects car activity from the observed anchor to the scenario evaluation year using the observed CAGR. Passenger light trucks receive the remainder of the combined total indexed to that same year.

!!! info "Survival curve tails are extrapolations"
    To meet Temoa's zero-endpoint requirement without dropping directly to zero at the end of the source schedule, retain the source values and, where needed, extend the final annual retirement rate until survival reaches the configured `tail_survival_threshold` (0.5% of the original cohort, by default). Retain that value and set survival to zero in the following year. The extended tail is an assumption, not additional source evidence.

!!! warning "Extrapolated survival curves can extend well beyond observed ages"
    Tail extrapolations can be manually capped, the default `survival_curve_max_age: null` derives each source's final rate from this rule. An explicit age shortens a longer derived curve by forcing zero at that age and reports
    removed surviving stock (if any). Extrapolated survival curves can be long, the [*EIA NEMS*](https://github.com/EIAgov/NEMS/blob/main/input/tdm/trnhdvx.xlsx) heavy-truck schedule extends beyond age 140 under this assumption, ignored by the model horizon.

!!! warning "VKT tails hold final observed mileage"
    Available [*NLR ATB*](https://atb.nlr.gov/transportation/2024/data) mileage profiles end at unequal ages (25 to 33 years). Hold each native class's final mileage before weighted aggregation; this is an assumption, not additional age evidence. Fixed-lifetime schedules include every operating period before retirement and average only its operating years in a partial final period. With survival curves, VKT profiles extend through the model horizon, bounded by each process's retirement endpoint.

## `efficiency`

!!! info "Aggregate consumption before taking its inverse"
    Consumption and service efficiency use reciprocal relationships. Fleet, range and fuel-component weights must keep their original physical meaning; see [fuel consumption and service efficiency](model/energy.md).

The LDV ratings and truck fleet pathways align their [*NRCan Ratings*](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/98f1a129-f628-4ce4-b24d-6f16bf24dd64) or [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) consumption evidence with [*NLR ATB*](https://atb.nlr.gov/transportation/2024/data) in 2023 on the default grid. The forward bridge multiplies the aligned consumption anchor by ATB consumption at the target divided by ATB consumption in 2023. The 2024 model-year target uses a linear interpolation of native ATB 2023 and 2025 consumption. Sparse ratings after 2023 remain diagnostic evidence. The annual audit retains the requested year, source points and applied weights.

For LDVs and medium/heavy trucks, use raw cohort stock to weight the latest eligible observed bin and anchor new-vehicle projections. In each new-vintage window, give unsupported annual ratings zero weight and renormalize the supported years.

Pair observed consumption with its annual load and projected consumption with the weighted anchor load. Buses and motorcycles use annual or base-year CEUD load, as applicable. Calculate period efficiency as aggregated load divided by aggregated consumption, expressed as energy per vehicle-kilometer.

- **Medium and heavy trucks.** Incumbent fuels use their CEUD fleet consumption.
  Emerging powertrain estimates scale annual diesel fleet consumption by the
  aligned 2023 ATB powertrain-to-diesel consumption ratio. Projected medium-truck
  gasoline consumption keeps its own CEUD anchor and uses the ATB diesel trend.
- **Buses.** Backcasts multiply aligned ATB consumption by the CEUD annual-to-reference
  ratios for service intensity and load. Intercity buses use the transit diesel
  anchor scaled by the applicable REGEN consumption ratio, with transit ATB HEV
  as the hybrid proxy. These historical estimates carry their proxy or backcast
  treatment in the audit. The CEUD bus backcast reference is the scenario base
  year. A later base year uses the configured CEUD hold or extrapolation.
- **Motorcycles.** CEUD supplies the incumbent service intensity and load.
  GCAM target-to-gasoline consumption ratios index the projected values at
  the selected native points. For projected years the CEUD intensity and load
  use the scenario base year, with the configured hold or extrapolation when
  that year is beyond the CEUD observation. An emerging BEV inside the historical part of
  the new-vintage window uses an explicit GCAM relative-powertrain proxy.
- **Fixed PHEV input split.** The shared annual blend uses the mean of the selected
  ATB component shares at the model interval ends. It applies to every period
  because the blend is shared across vintages. The sample dates remain
  2030–2050 on the five-year grid in both modes. Fuel and electricity remain
  separate physical energy components.

| Road vehicles – load factors | [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) activity, distance and stock describe whole classes. | Derive occupancy/payload as `activity ÷ (avg. distance × stock)`. For LDVs and medium/heavy trucks, use anchor-consumption cohort weights and hold that factor for future vehicles; other road classes retain the base-year factor. |

!!! warning "Lower gasoline consumption may reflect the medium-truck fleet mix"
    Gasoline medium trucks use less energy per vehicle-km in the
    [Ontario incumbent comparison](model/energy.md#ldv-eff-8-medium-trucks-incumbents-mj-vkm).
    This build assigns both fuels the same payload within each vintage, so gasoline
    also delivers more modeled tonne-km per MJ. One plausible explanation for the
    consumption gap is a larger share of Class 2b–3 vehicles in the gasoline fleet
    and more trucks configured for heavier loads in the diesel fleet. The aggregate
    CEUD evidence leaves the weight-class mix and typical payload by fuel unobserved.

    To test lower gasoline freight efficiency in the model, start by reviewing
    [capacity-to-activity (`C2A`) factors](#capacity_to_activity) by powertrain to
    reflect gasoline trucks' potentially lower payload. C2A scales annual service
    per vehicle. Use payload evidence in the efficiency calculation as well,
    because tonnes carried per vehicle divided by MJ per vehicle-km determines
    tonne-km per MJ. Recheck the class activity calibration with the revised
    C2A factors.

### Reviewed vehicle mappings

The [canonical vehicle table](../config/parameters/vehicle_reference.csv) records vehicle identities and their supported years, classes and powertrains. The [source-link table](../config/parameters/vehicle_source_links.csv) records source labels and uncertainties. The backend reads both through a validated loader without changing them. NRCan Ratings supplies the vehicle catalogue; historical-only identities and unsupported years remain explicitly recorded. Wards sales data support some mapping decisions.

For each model year, the backend calculates median energy consumption and electric range across all distinct compatible configurations. For PHEVs, range means electric-driving range. It retains the existing range buckets, bucket-specific utility factors, geographic and historical proxies, and vehicle-count totals. A model’s available powertrains do not reveal the powertrain of each observed vehicle, so HEV estimates use catalogue medians rather than Ontario MTO weights. The EPA configuration comparison does not assign vehicle identities.
### Optional [*NRCan CEUD*](https://oee.nrcan.gc.ca/corporate/statistics/neud/dpa/menus/trends/comprehensive_tables/list.cfm) reported liquid-energy calibration {#optional-ceud-reported-liquid-energy-calibration}

!!! warning "One factor cannot identify technology-specific errors"
    This optional calibration is off by default. For each supported region and vehicle class, it adjusts consumption by one factor so estimated gasoline and diesel use matches reported CEUD energy for the reference-year fleet and activity. Propane, natural gas and unresolved fuel differences remain separate; missing electricity data is not treated as zero. Heavy-truck CEUD energy is treated as entirely diesel.

    The same factor also applies to all powertrains, ranges and future years, including electricity, although the fit uses liquid-fuel evidence. It cannot identify which technologies cause a discrepancy. Unadjusted consumption, the PHEV blend and source evidence are retained.

    Calibration requires fixed road lifetimes and flat annual utilization. The [method and equation](model/energy.md#optional-ceud-energy-calibration) and [calibration notebook](insights/ceud_calibration_diagnosis.py) explain the results and limitations. Matching reported energy is not independent validation.

| LDVs – glider refresh guard | `#edge-case` Identical saved [*ANL GREET2*](https://greet.anl.gov/greet/versions.html) inputs may diverge after refresh. | Preserve native formulas and automated range selectors. Recheck saved inputs on refresh; require a corrected downstream selector if range assumptions diverge. |
| LDVs – lightweight BEV gliders | `#edge-case` [*ANL GREET2*](https://greet.anl.gov/greet/versions.html)'s native range switch disagrees with adjacent labels. | AER150/200/300/400 selects EV400/EV150/EV200/EV300 gliders. `W4`/`Y4`/`AA4`/`AC4` inputs are identical within `Car`, `SUV` and `PUT` sheets in the registered release, so the mismatch currently has no numerical effect and lightweight factors remain accepted. |
| Medium and heavy trucks | [*ANL GREET*](https://greet.anl.gov/greet/versions.html) supplies Class 6 and Class 8 Day/Sleeper models. | Use Class 6 for medium trucks, preserving scenario GVWR weights; aggregate heavy trucks with regional DayCab/Sleeper weights. HEV/PHEV use HEV; FCEV/FCHEV use FCV. These factors have no material scenario and do not use LDV selectors. |

!!! note "Manufacturing totals are not annual operating emissions"
    Vehicle-cycle factors apply to newly manufactured vehicles. Do not divide lifetime mass totals by mileage or lifetime; preserve each gas's units and the selected vehicle-cycle boundary.

## `limit_new_capacity_share` and representative LDV ranges

Owners: [*EPA OMEGA source*](../config/sources.yaml), [`ldv_ev_ranges` rules](../config/parameters/charging_and_range_representation.yaml) and [default range selection](../config/scenarios/default.yaml).

| Technology class | Source / challenge | Assumption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LDVs – new range shares | Unconstrained short-range options dominate deployment. | In `new_capacity_shares`, retain all variants/parameters; add family groups/membership and constant [*EPA OMEGA*](https://github.com/USEPA/EPA_OMEGA_Model) minimum shares (`≥`). Complete shares sum to one, fixing the within-family range mix without fixing BEV/PHEV adoption. |
| LDVs – range evidence | [*EPA OMEGA*](https://github.com/USEPA/EPA_OMEGA_Model) US MY2022 baseline is model evidence, not current Canadian sales. | Use sales-weighted charge-depleting range distributions within class/powertrain. These are range shares, not BEV/PHEV adoption shares. |
| LDVs – regional/time transfer | [*EPA OMEGA*](https://github.com/USEPA/EPA_OMEGA_Model) lacks Canadian and future-specific weights. | Use its car mix for cars and light-truck mix for passenger/freight light trucks. Hold shares across regions and future vintages. |
| LDVs – representative archetypes | `#multi-parameter` One technology replaces each BEV/PHEV range family. | In `BEV_PHEV_range_representation`: `representative_archetype`, weight consumption, costs and lifetime embodied mass with the same [*EPA OMEGA*](https://github.com/USEPA/EPA_OMEGA_Model) mix; derive efficiency from weighted consumption. Emit no range groups or new-capacity-share constraints; see [efficiency](#efficiency) and [investment](#cost_invest). |
| LDVs – representative PHEV fuel shares | `#edge-case` A shared supporting blend cannot distinguish vehicle vintages. | Average each category's consumption-weighted fuel/electricity shares equally across model vintages; apply to all vintages/periods. Total energy is conserved; component fuel use remains approximate. |
!!! warning "Range composition does not determine adoption"
    Within-family range shares or representative archetypes restrict range detail while leaving BEV/PHEV adoption to the optimizer. A shared PHEV blend preserves total energy with reported component approximations.
