Main figure. Technology structure of the legacy CANOE transportation model, including its embedded energy-supply layer. Imported fuels undergo emissions accounting and, where applicable, blending before use by transport technologies. Electric vehicles use class-specific charging infrastructure; plug-in hybrids use liquid-fuel/electricity blends. Hydrogen is produced through steam methane reforming (with or without carbon capture and storage) or alkaline/PEM electrolysis, then distributed, compressed, stored and delivered through refuelling infrastructure. Vehicle families supply 14 passenger/freight demand commodities and one off-road energy demand. The energy trunk groups eligible routes; it does not connect every carrier to every mode. GHG signals represent upstream and direct fuel emissions plus the separately recorded SMR-with-CCS process emissions. External electricity interfaces E_elc_dx and I_elc are preserved without inventing an internal producer. The original vehicle-cycle parameter annotation is a schema capability: EmissionEmbodied is empty in the inspected legacy database.

Companion figure. Carrier-to-mode eligibility derived from positive Efficiency links in the compiled legacy transport SQLite. Filled cells indicate at least one technology path, including charging and PHEV blends. Hydrogen is classified at vehicle input, rather than by its production feedstock. Counts include existing/new and range variants, exclude supporting supply technologies, and refer to this specific compiled database rather than the older counts printed in the supplied diagram. Service connections include the legacy annual-to-demand dummy bridges.

Abbreviations: GSL, gasoline blendstock; ETH, ethanol; DSL, diesel; RDSL, renewable diesel; JTF, conventional jet fuel; SPK, synthetic paraffinic kerosene; CNG/LNG, compressed/liquefied natural gas; HFO, heavy fuel oil; MDO, marine diesel oil; ELC, electricity; SMR, steam methane reforming; CCS, carbon capture and storage; ALK, alkaline; PEM, proton exchange membrane; LDV/MDV/HDV, light-/medium-/heavy-duty vehicles; BEV, battery electric vehicle; PHEV, plug-in hybrid electric vehicle.
